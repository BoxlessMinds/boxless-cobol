Hall of PICtures

In COBOL, every variable has a PICTURE: a little drawing of exactly what it holds. 9 is a digit, X is any character, V is an invisible decimal point. The curators have framed the finest examples.

PIC 9(5)V99

Untitled (Salary)Oil on mainframe, 1974 Five digits, then an implied point, then two more. Holds up to 99999.99 in seven bytes. The point is never stored. The program just knows where it goes.

PIC X(30)

Portrait of a Customer NamePadded with spaces Thirty characters, always. "Ada" is stored as "Ada" plus 27 spaces. COBOL never asks how long a string is. It already knows.

PIC S9(7) COMP-3

Packed DecimalAbstract period Two digits squeezed into every byte, with the sign tucked into the last half-byte. Seven digits fit in four bytes. When disk space cost a fortune, this was a masterpiece.

PIC $$$,$$9.99

Still Life with Floating Dollar SignEdited picture For printing, not math. The dollar sign "floats" right to sit next to the first real digit, so 42 prints as     $42.00. Cheque printers loved it.
