Exhibit: You Used COBOL Today

8:02 AM

The cash machine

You take out cash. The ATM talks to newer systems, but at many banks the account record that actually changes is kept by a decades-old COBOL batch or transaction program.

Friday

The paycheck

Hours times rate, plus overtime, minus tax, grouped by department: payroll is the job COBOL was built for. This repo even has one (see below).

Monthly

The insurance letter

Policies, premiums and claims are fixed-width records processed in nightly batches. Many insurers still run that on a mainframe.

The 2020 Call for Help

Spring 2020

When the pandemic hit, unemployment claims in the US jumped almost overnight. Several states found their benefit systems ran on COBOL, and the governor of New Jersey went on television to ask for volunteers who knew the language. Retired programmers answered. So did a lot of curious new learners.

Tell me more

The real bottleneck was often capacity and the systems around the COBOL code, not the language itself. But the moment made headlines, and free courses (like the Open Mainframe Project one in the Reading Room) saw a rush of sign-ups.

The Stat Wall

All figures are estimates. Read the labels.

  • 220 billion lines of COBOL in use. Estimate, Reuters, 2017.
  • 95% of ATM card swipes rely on COBOL code. Estimate, Reuters, 2017.
  • 43% of banking systems built on COBOL. Estimate, Reuters, 2017.

Nobody can count every line of COBOL in the world, so treat these as "a lot", "nearly all" and "nearly half". The real numbers move slowly as systems are replaced.
